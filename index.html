about me

I'm a third-year Applied Mathematics student at Universitas Pelita Harapan, specializing in Business Mathematics, with a deep interest in quantitative finance and the challenge of finding structure in messy, unpredictable financial data.

I work where mathematical rigor meets real markets, building forecasting models, running event studies, and letting data settle questions that intuition cannot. As I grow, I aspire to build a career in finance or other data-driven fields, applying mathematical modeling and analytical thinking to problems that matter.

Currently, I'm conducting my undergraduate thesis on how geopolitical conflicts affect energy-sector stock performance across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand, using a longitudinal event-study approach. I'm also preparing a conference paper on oil and gold price forecasting, exploring how statistical and machine learning methods can sharpen prediction in financial markets.

Beyond research, I've worked as a Teaching Assistant and currently work part-time in an marketing division, alongside leadership roles across student organizations. These experiences have strengthened my communication, teamwork, and project management skills.

Analysis of Longitudinal Responses of Energy Stocks to Five Global Geopolitical Conflicts: A Comparison Across Subsectors and Countries in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand

Undergraduate Thesis · Universitas Pelita Harapan · 2026

This research examines how five major geopolitical conflicts influenced the performance of energy-sector stocks across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand using a longitudinal event-study approach. By comparing market reactions across countries and subsectors, it provides insights into the impact of geopolitical risk on the financial markets of Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand.

02

Forecasting Oil and Gold Prices with Regime and Structural Demand Features

Conference Paper · ICTIIA 2026

This research evaluates machine learning and statistical models for one-step-ahead forecasting of crude oil and gold prices, incorporating regime indicators and structural demand features. Linear Regression, XGBoost, and Random Forest are compared, with XGBoost delivering the best predictive performance while highlighting the challenges of incorporating long-term structural demand signals into short-horizon forecasting.
